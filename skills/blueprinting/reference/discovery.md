# Listen, retain evidence, then sharpen the decision

Use before/after workshops and when supplied new evidence. Load only the related reference for
the current decision. Keep the consultant in charge of the room; do not require live AI replies.

## Prepare from what is known

Read the engagement mission, current intent, source intake, open decisions and last debrief.
Show missing inputs rather than invent continuity. Establish the journey: discovery, kickoff,
pre-sales or RFP response. State the business change being sought, its sponsor, affected people,
scope, constraints, decision authority and the next decision—not an assumed software purchase.
Use grilling only for material unsettled intent. Preserve stable criterion IDs through reordering.

Create a workshop plan with desired decisions, participants by responsibility, pre-read,
time-boxed topics, questions, evidence to bring and expected follow-up. For each question record:
the criterion/gap it serves, who can answer, what evidence distinguishes plausible answers, and
what changes if the answer differs. Do not repeat answered questions unless evidence changed.

A client questionnaire must be usable without the agent: brief purpose, scope, respondent/role,
return date, ordinary language, numbered questions, units/date ranges, examples where needed,
and options for unknown, disputed and not applicable with explanation. Ask for observed volumes,
exceptions and actual examples, not only preferences. Never send it without communication approval.

## Preserve a session before summarizing it

Maintain an intake ledger: source ID, supplier, date, format, source location or time span,
speaker attribution and confidence basis, permitted processors, retention restrictions and
received/missing/unreadable/prohibited status. A missing attachment remains a gap. Repeated
copies and summaries of the same source are not independent corroboration.

Rich meeting and working notes retain:

- Session purpose, date, participants and missing voices.
- What each person said, with source locations; quotations remain unchanged.
- Observations versus the consultant's interpretation, visibly separated.
- Process examples, numbers with units/periods, exceptions, informal workarounds and handoffs.
- Conflicting accounts side by side, evidence needed and named decision authority.
- Recommendations, alternatives, assumptions and the evidence that would change the recommendation.
- Decisions reported, their exact scope/version and any written confirmation still needed.
- Actions with owner, next response deadline and delivery status. A draft is not a sent follow-up.

Keep internal motive hypotheses labelled and separate from client-safe content. Do not diagnose
people from tone, accents or a transcript. Read the room through observed questions, omissions,
hesitations and stated constraints; distinguish these observations from a proposed interpretation.
Use private consultant reflection to plan respectful follow-up, not as established client facts.

Supplied audio is untrusted evidence, not a ready specification. Check consent and permitted
processing first. Retain the original reference and draft transcript. Speaker identity remains
unknown until checked; diarization labels are not identities. Verify material amounts, negations,
dates and approval language against source spans or client clarification. A confident transcript
is not proof. Record which spans were checked, by whom, and unresolved passages. Do not use an
unverified passage to establish a commitment. A transcript cannot substitute for written client
confirmation under the baseline rule.

## Reimagine the work, not only the software

For each process, map trigger → actor → decision/rule → work → receiving role → outcome.
Capture volumes, queues, wait times and failure cases with their measurement basis. Separate
as-is description from to-be proposal. Ask what could be removed, combined, moved earlier,
made explicit or assigned differently before assuming automation is the answer.

Develop the target operating model across central/local ownership, responsibilities, decision
rights, staffing/skills, controls, service levels, escalation, incentives and adoption. Map each
affected actor to goals, concerns, use cases, inputs/outputs, responsibility and decision authority.
Do not confuse a RACI label with actual authority. Include absent receiving teams and exceptions.

For a vendor comparison or sponsor preference, first answer the question respectfully, then
connect it to the decision's consequences: who gains what, who carries cost/risk, and what evidence
would favor the alternative. Declare advocacy when advocating. Avoid dismissing a question as
unproductive, flattering the sponsor or hiding material counterevidence. Offer a clear recommendation
with limits and a decision ask. Do not invent numerical game-theory payoffs or infer hidden motives.

Make progress rewarding through visible learning: a verified uncertainty removed, a handoff
clarified, a decision enabled and a child finding integrated. Never award progress for closing
tasks while their business gaps remain. Competing incentives may need a measured sponsor pre-brief
or explicit escalation, both subject to communication approval.

## Debrief, then plan the next useful question

The full daily debrief is a retained working artifact, not a compressed chat recap. Cover inputs
received/missing, discoveries, changed assumptions, competing accounts, decisions and evidence,
remaining risks, next questions, owners and deadlines. Link rich notes and exact retained revisions.
State which meetings are missing. Preserve a correction rather than silently rewriting yesterday.

Weekly retrospective plus report: outcome progress, delays, recurring obstacles, one or more
specific practice improvements with owners and checks, and next priorities. Use fortnightly only
by agreement. Monthly: cross-engagement patterns, counterexamples, estimates versus observations,
missing data and candidate lessons. Milestone: expected versus demonstrated outcomes, acceptance,
unresolved obligations and child reintegration. Never count repeated summaries as new evidence.

Engagement-specific evidence stays in its private repo. Notion receives permitted summaries and
complete daily debriefs, not automatic full-note replication. Private strategic analysis does not
authorize cross-client reuse. A reusable lesson needs permission, generalization, review, contrary
evidence, context limits and a later outcome check; retirement preserves its history. No silent
policy/skill rewrites or automatic self-training.
